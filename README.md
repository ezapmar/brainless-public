It runs on your own machine. A folder of plain text notes, a handful of small scripts and
whichever AI model you point them at. No server, no account, nothing to sign up for. Your
notes stay in your folder, on your disk, in a format you could still open in twenty
years.

Sherlock had the method. Watson wrote it down, asked the obvious question, and kept the
files. This is the Watson. You keep the method.

---

## The problem

My notes were scattered across six places. Voice notes to myself in the car. Screenshots
on the phone. Meeting transcripts in e-mail. Articles I meant to return to. Half a
thought typed into a chat window at midnight. Each one was written down somewhere and
then effectively gone, because "I wrote that down" and "I can find it again" are not the
same sentence. I lost weeks to hunting for things I already knew.

The second problem was worse. I run a company, so my days are decisions, most of them
made on a phone between two meetings and never looked at again. That is how you get to be
wrong the same way twice without noticing.

A pile of notes does not fix it. Notes that nobody reads are a warehouse. Summaries that
nobody argues with are a tidier warehouse. And an argument that nobody grades is
entertainment, and I already have football for that.

## The solution

One loop, and each part is there because the one before it is useless alone. Collect
everything without judging it. Let the machine read it and make the connections. Argue
with what you are about to decide. Write the decision down as a bet, and settle the bet
later. Then keep the promises you made along the way.

The bet underneath it all is connectivity. A note on its own is storage. Notes wired to
each other are a map, and the value shows up when you move across it: an argument from a
meeting in May lands beside a belief written in March, and the two change each other.

The notes were a warehouse. What I wanted was a colleague.

### 1. Gather information

**Everything comes in through one door.** A voice note, a photo of a handwritten page, a
link, a PDF, a meeting transcript, a line typed on the phone. It all becomes a note in the
same folder. Nothing is tagged, filed or judged on the way in. Judging a thought at
capture time is how good ones die in a car park.

A voice note comes back as text, transcribed on my own machine, so a thought from the car
is no longer gone by Friday. Years of chat history can come in too, filtered first, because most of it is lookups and
the rest is the most private thing I own.

**Then the machine reads it so I do not have to.** A pile of captures is only useful if
somebody reads it, and I was never going to be that somebody. Every note is summarised
and linked to the people, companies and ideas it mentions. An idea that keeps coming back
gets a page of its own, and every new source improves that page instead of piling up
beside it. When two sources disagree, the page keeps both, with dates. A claim that went
out of date is struck through, not deleted, because what I used to believe and why is the
part a search engine cannot give me. Nobody has to spend a Sunday curating a knowledge
graph.

**The connections get made for me.** A note from last year turns up beside the thing I am
looking at today because they share a subject, not because I remembered to link them. I
do not make the links any more, and the links are better for it, because the system has
no favourites and never conveniently forgets the note that undermines the plan I am
attached to.

### 2. Think deeper and clearer

**Ask what you already know.** Before anything hard, one search answers "what have I
already written about this", including the parts I had forgotten writing. Every answer
cites the page it came from and ends with what the vault does not cover. Before a
meeting, a short brief. Overnight, a digest of the day. And every night the same
twenty-odd questions with known answers are asked again, so I learn that search got
worse from a number, not from a bad answer on a bad day.

**Then let it argue.** Before a real decision, `/decide` reads what I believe and what I
decided before, quotes the beliefs that bear on the question word for word, and makes me
write criteria before options, so a favourite cannot write its own test. It starts with
one question: if this is wrong, what does undoing it cost? A cheap, reversible decision
gets three lines and a "decide today". Only a one-way door gets the full framework.

Every night each new source is also read against its nearest pages, and when it
contradicts an old note, a decision or a project page, I hear about it in the morning.
On Sunday the week ends with one question, drafted from what I wrote, that I answer in a
thread.

There used to be six personas here, each built out of a book on thinking, arguing a
thesis in two rounds. They ran from September to November 2026 and were retired when the
whole thing moved onto one Mac. The books are still on the shelf, in
[References](docs/references.md), and still shape `/decide`.

Nothing here makes you smarter. It changes what you have to write down before you may
move on, and the order you see things in. Criteria before options. A number before a
feeling. One dated action at the end, so a decision cannot pass as an opinion.

### 3. Decide

Reading is still not thinking. The machine can tell you what you wrote, but not what you
hold to be true, so that part is written by hand. A belief carries a line for "what would
change my mind". A decision carries a prediction, a confidence and a review date.

The format matters more than it looks. A bet can lose, and something that can lose can be
argued with.

It never picks for you. When a review date passes, it nags until you write down what
actually happened. Ten years of ungraded decisions is one year repeated ten times. If you
do only this step, you are ahead of most people I know. Including me, for most of this
year.

Every answer, graded or not, is filed back into the vault. That is where the loop closes,
because tomorrow's question gets to build on today's answer.

### 4. Bonus: get shit done

Decisions come with promises attached, and promises are the first thing to fall out of a
busy head.

- **Promises become tasks by themselves.** What I say I will do in a meeting or a voice
  note is picked up, checked against what is already on the list so the same job is not
  written down three times in three different wordings, and synced with Google Tasks both
  ways.
- **Every morning, at most three things.** A decision due for grading, the oldest promise
  still open, and one piece of evidence worth a second look, each with a link to where it
  came from. Answer it, push it to a date, or drop it with a reason.
- **It asks, I answer, in a thread.** Reminders, the morning three and the weekly
  question arrive on my phone. I reply to the message itself. "Apply" writes the note,
  "defer" and a date moves it. Nothing is written into my notes until I
  say so.

## How I use it on an ordinary day

- **All day, with no effort.** I send things to a Telegram bot on my phone: a voice note
  in the car, a photo of a whiteboard, a link. That is the whole capture ritual. No
  folders, no tags, no inbox to process later.
- **Before anything hard.** I ask the bot. A message that starts with `?` is a question to
  the vault, answered from my own pages with the page names, on the model on my Mac.
- **Morning.** The night is already compiled. At most three things are waiting.
- **Overnight, without me.** At 23:00 the Mac digests the day, rebuilds everything, looks
  for contradictions and makes one commit. If the lid was shut, it runs when it wakes.

## How it runs

One Mac, one folder, four background jobs, one chat with a bot. Since 15 November 2026
there is no second machine, no relay and no VPN. I had three devices and two schedulers,
and the setup had become the hobby.

```
iPhone / iPad ──Telegram──▶ Mac  capture (every 2 min)
                                  ├─ plain message → a note
                                  ├─ reply to bot  → approve, defer, follow up
                                  └─ "?question"   → a cited answer, local model
iPhone / iPad ◀─iCloud────▶ Mac  the vault, opened in Obsidian
                            Mac  hourly: tasks, mail (Spiky + Gmail "Brain" label),
                                 meeting brief, morning set (model-free briefing after 07:00)
                            Mac  23:00: ingest, digest, compile, index, links, lint
                                 (orphans, broken, dead-ends), attention audit,
                                 Sunday weeklies (dreaming, kill review, weekly archive),
                                 health, one commit and push
                            Mac  always: a local model on 127.0.0.1:8080
GitHub (private)                 the off-site copy, one push a night
```

| Job | When | Script | Writes git? |
|---|---|---|---|
| capture | every 2 minutes | `.agents/scripts/capture.sh` | no |
| hourly | every hour | `.agents/scripts/cron_wrapper.sh` | no |
| night | 23:00, or on wake if missed | `.agents/scripts/night.sh` | yes, once |
| llm | always on | `llama-server` | no |

- **One writer.** Only the night shift commits, so there is no lock and no conflict path.
  launchd never starts a second copy of a running job.
- **One channel.** Everything the system says to me goes through `tools/notify.py`, a
  durable outbox on the bot: queued before it is sent, retried, sent at most once. The
  first line of a message is its label (`#tasks`, `#thinking`, `#dreaming`, `#ops`), and
  my reply is routed by the label of the message it answers.
- **Two models, one routing file.** Eight short or private lanes (`note-classify`,
  `dreaming-judge`, `compile-aliases`, `compile-local`, `capture-note`, `capture-photo`,
  `spiky-actions`, `ask`) run on Gemma 4 12B on the Mac, configured in `brainless.toml`
  and pinned in its fail-closed `local_only` list. If the local server is down, or if an
  override tries to send a local lane to the cloud, the call is refused and the text stays
  home. The pages everything else reads (compile, digests, weekly synthesis) are written by
  Claude on a subscription, because a weak writer caps what any reader can get back. The
  morning briefing itself (`tools/morning_briefing.py`) builds with no model at all, so a
  closed desktop app no longer means a missing morning.
- **The phone needs no engine.** It opens the vault from iCloud Drive in Obsidian and
  talks to the bot. That is all.

The whole system on one page, with every job and lane: [Architecture](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

## What it changed

I stopped carrying it all in my head, which was the whole point.

The part that pays for the whole thing: the first run, on 9 September 2026, found that my
argument against a partner's equity ask was aimed at the wrong object. I would have walked
into that meeting confidently wrong, which is the expensive way to be wrong.

## Keep it yours

A loop that runs unattended against a folder holding your life needs one rule above all
the others: nothing writes into your notes without you. The machine proposes and you
apply.

- Everything runs on your own hardware. Three privacy layers sit in front of any cloud
  model call: pattern redaction (`tools/redact.py`), folder and frontmatter exclusion
  (`cloud_exclude` in `PROFILE.md` and `privacy: personal` notes compile locally on
  `compile-local`; `privacy: secret` notes are never compiled; `tools/llm.py` refuses a
  cloud call on an excluded path), and an egress ledger (`.agents/state/egress.jsonl`)
  that `health_check.py` turns red on drift.
- Folders you mark protected, like health or identity papers, stay out of git and out of
  cloud models.
- The rules are checks, not requests. `raw/` is strictly read-only (`Edit(raw/**)` and
  `Write(raw/**)` are denied in `.claude/settings.json` and `claude_guard.py`) so clipped
  text cannot talk an agent into rewriting your sources. A write into your own folders
  asks you first. A deletion asks. A secret or a bank number never lands in a file. A page
  where the model talked about itself instead of your source is refused.
- What you teach one agent, every agent knows. Your standing preferences and the lessons
  from correcting an agent live in one file in the vault, `_Agent-Context/LEARNINGS.md`,
  not in one tool's memory, so the chat agents, the nightly jobs and whatever you use next
  all read the same list.
- Every Sunday the whole vault, history included, is sealed with a key the machine cannot
  open and put somewhere sync does not reach (`tools/vault_archive.py`). A restore test
  proves it comes back. Sync is not backup: a bad night copies itself everywhere in minutes.
- The engine and the notes are separate, so you can share one and keep the other. This
  public repo contains no notes at all.

## Where this came from

On the evening of 8 September 2026 I sent myself two voice notes from the car. The first
said the notes I take all day should argue with each other at night. The second said this
thing should be installable by someone who is not me.

I had the vault already. I had beliefs written down. I had decision notes with a "review
date" field that nobody ever reviewed, least of all me. So I put five books on a shelf and
turned each one into a voice that reads my day and pushes back. The first round ran the
next day at 12:30. Five voices, ten replies, one synthesis, one bet. The sixth voice came
later.

The name came earlier, and it is a joke that stuck. I kept telling the company "let us
use our own brain first, then we can talk about a second brain", so when the folder
needed a name I typed brainless: not a second brain, zero brain. It turned out to carry
the design rule. The system keeps the memory and the connections. The thinking stays
yours.

## Who this is for

People who make decisions for a living and suspect their own reasoning. Founders,
operators, product people, anyone whose calendar is a sequence of judgement calls.

It helps if you already write things down. It helps more if you are willing to be told
you are wrong by a script overnight.

## What it is not

- Not a chat app, although it is getting conversational. The notes are still the record.
  The threads are how the record gets made.
- Not a knowledge graph you maintain. The graph is built for you, not curated by you.
  It does get measured: how many pages nothing links to, how many hang together, which
  pages hold two clusters together. The numbers go into the health report every week.
- Not a decision maker. It never picks. Duke would call that resulting in advance.
- Not finished. I built it for one person, made it installable, then cut it back to
  the one machine I actually use. Expect rough edges, and tell me where they are.

## Install

It runs on macOS. You need git, Homebrew, Python 3.13 and a Telegram bot. The four jobs
are launchd agents; nothing here is a Windows or Linux service any more.

```bash
git clone https://github.com/ezapmar/brainless-public.git ~/projects/brainless
cd ~/projects/brainless
python3.13 -m venv ~/.local/venvs/brainless
~/.local/venvs/brainless/bin/pip install -r requirements-core.txt -r requirements-search.txt -r requirements-google.txt
```

The vault path defaults to `~/projects/brainless`; set `BRAINLESS_VAULT` to put it
elsewhere. Then:

1. **Who you are.** Fill `_Agent-Context/PROFILE.md`: your name, the language it should
   write in (`en` or `tr`), `protected_homes` (folders that must never reach git) and
   `cloud_exclude` (folders compiled only on your local model). Put health records and
   identity papers in those lists before your first commit.
2. **The bot.** Make one with BotFather, then put its token in
   `~/.config/brainless/telegram_token` and your chat id in
   `~/.config/brainless/telegram_chat_id`. Only that chat can write to the vault.
3. **The writer model.** Install the Claude CLI and sign in with your subscription. Any
   other backend in `tools/llm.py` works for a lane too (`python3 tools/llm.py --lanes`).
4. **The local model and routing.** `brew install llama.cpp`, put a Gemma 4 12B GGUF and
   its vision projector in `~/models/gemma-4-12b/` (or point `BRAINLESS_LOCAL_MODEL` and
   `BRAINLESS_LOCAL_MMPROJ` at yours), and copy `brainless.toml.example` to
   `brainless.toml`. Without the model file the jobs still install, and the local lanes
   fail closed until the file exists. `brainless.toml` is the one place for lane routing
   and `local_only`; move a lane only after checking it agrees with the big model on real
   samples ([Local Inference](docs/local-inference.md)).
5. **The jobs.**

   ```bash
   bash .agents/launchd/install.sh
   ```

   Run it again after changing a script path; `--uninstall` removes the jobs.

**Optional inputs.** Documents need nothing more: markitdown is installed with its PDF,
DOCX, XLSX and PPTX extras. Voice needs `ffmpeg` and
[whisper.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/whisper.cpp) with a model file (I run
`large-v3-turbo`); transcription stays on the Mac. Backups need
[age](https://github.com/FiloSottile/age): put the public key in `PROFILE.md` and keep the
private one in your password manager. Google Tasks and the meeting brief need a Google
OAuth client of your own. For e-mail capture, create a Gmail label named `Brain` and add
your IMAP app password in `~/.config/brainless/`: `.agents/scripts/gmail_brain_intake.py`
reads it read-only, tracks Message-IDs so nothing duplicates, and saves notes as
`privacy: personal` (compiled locally only). For browser clipping, import the three JSON
templates in [docs/clipper-templates/](docs/clipper-templates/) into Obsidian Web Clipper.

**Other agents (optional).** `tools/mcp_server.py` serves the compiled wiki read-only to
any MCP client: search, one page, the index. It never serves a page git ignores.

```bash
claude mcp add --scope user brainless-wiki -- ~/.local/venvs/brainless/bin/python ~/projects/brainless/tools/mcp_server.py
```

## The first ten minutes

Send the bot a line. Two minutes later it is a note in `Thinking/Daily/`. Send it a link
and it lands in `Inbox/Links/`. Send `?what do I know about hiring` and the answer comes
back with the pages it read.

To build the wiki now instead of at 23:00:

```bash
source .agents/scripts/env.sh
python3 tools/nightly_compile.py
python3 tools/wiki_search.py "the thing you keep thinking about"
```

The checks are only as good as what they have to check against, so give them something.
Open `_Templates/`. Copy `Belief.md` into `Thinking/Beliefs/` and write one thing you
believe about your work. Copy `Decision.md` into `Thinking/Decisions/` for the last real
decision you made, with a prediction and a date.

Inside Claude Code, from the vault root, seven slash commands and two agents live beside
your notes:

- `/decide`: a decision against your beliefs and past decisions, reversibility first.
- `/research`: a question researched in a fixed order, hypotheses before searching.
- `/weekly`: the weekly review.
- `/context`: a snapshot of where things stand, and where `CONTEXT.md` has drifted.
- `/lint`: wiki integrity checks (broken links, orphans, dead-ends) and repairs.
- `/reply`: turn a saved conversation or e-mail thread plus rough notes into a
  commitment-checked draft in `Writings/Drafts/`, keeping open decisions outside the draft.
- `/trace`: end-to-end traceability audit of one active project from raw sources through
  `.wiki/`, tasks, contradictions, attention gaps and drafts.
- Agents (`.claude/agents/`): `scout` (read-only explorer for `Inbox/`, `raw/` and
  `.wiki/`) and `librarian` (deep `.wiki/` curator enforcing privacy and edit caps).

Every slash command definition lives in `.wiki/_commands/`, one Markdown prompt each, and
the `.claude/commands/` wrappers only point back there, so they work with any agent that
can read files. Every answer is filed back into `.wiki/digests/queries/`. `_Templates/`
also includes `40 Questions.md` for an annual and decade review.

## Honest limits

- It runs on a Mac. The launchd jobs, the paths and the local model setup assume one.
  Version 0.9 ran on Windows and Linux too; that layer is gone.
- It assumes a Claude subscription for the writing and a machine that can hold a 12B model
  in memory for the private lanes. Other providers work per lane, but I have tested them
  less.
- It speaks English and Turkish out of the box. Another language is a new folder of
  labels.
- Some jobs are mine and will do nothing for you until configured: a CRM snapshot, a mail
  capture, Google Tasks. They skip quietly when their credentials are missing.
- I am not a decision scientist. I am an operator who got tired of being wrong in the
  same way twice.

## Contributing

Issues and pull requests are open. If a check gave you a bad answer, paste the
message. If a job broke on your machine, paste its log from `logs/`. Both are more useful
than a feature request.

The regression tests run offline, with no credentials and no model calls:

```bash
python3 tools/run_tests.py
```
