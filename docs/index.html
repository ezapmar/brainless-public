The loop

Four knots. Each one holds because of the one before it.

Unread notes are storage. Summaries nobody challenges are tidier storage. Here is the loop that turns a folder into a working partner.

göz · the eye

Collect

Voice memos, whiteboard photos, links, PDFs, Gmail "Brain" threads, and clipped articles land in one folder. Nothing is tagged or filed on entry. Filing at capture time is how good thoughts die in a car park.

hayat ağacı · the tree of life

Compile and link

Every night (or right away with /ingest), the compiler summarises sources into .wiki/, links recurring concepts, weights observed post-ship outcomes above untested plans, and checks the visible Obsidian graph for orphans and leaks.

koçboynuzu · the ram's horn

Argue

Before a real choice, /decide quotes what you believe and asks what undoing it costs. Overnight, new sources are checked against nearby pages for contradictions, and an attention audit compares stated priorities against what you actually worked on.

yıldız · the star

Grade

A decision is written as a bet with a prediction, confidence, and review date. When the date passes, the morning briefing nags until you record what actually happened, so you stop being wrong the same way twice.

How it runs

One Mac, four jobs, one Telegram chat.

No second machine, no chat relay, no VPN. I had three devices and two schedulers until the plumbing became the hobby. Everything now runs on one Mac, and only one job writes to git.

capture

every two minutes

Polls the Telegram bot. Plain messages become notes, replies approve or defer proposals, and messages starting with ? return a cited answer from your local model.

hourly

every hour

Tasks sync, Gmail "Brain" label intake, pre-meeting briefs, health check, and a model-free morning briefing after 07:00 that includes wiki changes and 7-day attention gaps.

night

23:00, or on wake

One ordered chain: ingest, digest, compile, search index, link suggestions, wiki lint, visible vault graph audit, attention audit, Sunday weeklies (dreaming, kill review, encrypted backup), then one commit and push.

llm

always on, 127.0.0.1:8080

Gemma 4 12B on llama-server. Eight short or private lanes are pinned in brainless.toml under local_only. If the server is down or misconfigured, the call fails closed and the text stays home.

Every outbound notice runs through a SQLite outbox (tools/notify.py): queued, retried with backoff, sent at most once per key.

Keep it yours

Hard checks on disk, before any prompt leaves the machine.

Three privacy layers

Pattern redaction strips e-mails, phones, IBANs and keys. cloud_exclude folders and privacy: personal notes compile locally only; privacy: secret skips compilation. Every call logs to an egress ledger checked daily.

Human vs machine areas

raw/ is strictly read-only. Human folders require explicit approval before an agent writes. The engine owns .wiki/ and can rebuild it from scratch at any time.

Graph leak guard

tools/vault_graph.py checks the Obsidian-visible vault every night. A tracked file linking into a gitignored folder turns system health red, and unexpected work/personal bridges get flagged.

Weekly encrypted archive

Every Sunday the full vault and git history are sealed with age outside the synced folder. Sync copies mistakes everywhere in minutes; an encrypted archive waits quietly until you need it.

Set it up

An afternoon on a Mac. Two minutes to the first note.

Needs git, Homebrew, Python 3.13, a Telegram bot and the Claude CLI. Fill in _Agent-Context/PROFILE.md, copy brainless.toml.example to brainless.toml, put bot secrets in ~/.config/brainless, add a Gemma 4 12B model for local lanes, then install the four jobs.

Get the engine
git clone https://github.com/ezapmar/brainless-public.git ~/projects/brainless
Install the jobs
bash .agents/launchd/install.sh

Rerun after changing paths; --uninstall removes the jobs. Want zero daemons instead? Use brainless-lite.

The name is a joke that stuck.

In the office I kept saying "let us use our own brain first, then we can talk about a second brain", so when the folder needed a name I typed brainless. Not a second brain, zero brain. It ended up carrying the design rule anyway: the scripts keep the memory and the links; the thinking stays yours.

The mark is a brain drawn as a kilim. The motifs come from the looms of Anatolia and the steppe: the eye, the tree of life, the ram's horn, the star, the figure with hands on hips. Notes are the motifs; links are the threads.
